# Cello Synthesis — the adversarial judgment + the durable core

*Task #42. Judge and synthesist for 8 independent strikes at ONE problem: the most elegant
 PERVASIVE VISUAL COMPRESSION of the universal C** data model. The owner's caution governs:
  "don't take it too naively — this is a ship that's gotta sail very far." So this is not a
   beauty contest. Each strike is steelmanned, then sunk against the long voyage; then the
    durable core is named. No code was edited.*

## 0. What to get on with next

The verdict, in one breath: **the eight strikes are eight TEMPLATES, and they are all groping
 toward one MECHANISM they don't have — render the RESIDUAL (prediction error), not the
  particle.** Every strike that "draws each particle in full, however elegantly" has a hard
   scale cliff at ~10³–10⁴ particles, because it spends ink per particle and the expected is
    never cheap. The meditation (`Cello_meditation_todo.md`) already named the hinge; this doc
     confirms it survives the adversarial pass and turns it into a buildable first slice.

Get on with, in order:
- **Prove the residual atom** (§R.5, the "smallest buildable slice"): one particle drawn as its
   DEVIATION from a per-mainkey prior — a normal `%Record` a near-invisible mote, a wounded one
    (undef sc / .c foam / undropped-finished req) blazing to a full glyph, by the SAME ink rule.
     This is the one experiment that decides everything; build it before any full field.
- **Author the prior as matter**: a `%Norm:<mainkey>` under `The/Styles` beside Matstyle's
   swatches (§R.3). Bootstrap it by observation (the running snap IS the training set), let it be
    hand-corrected, let it snap. If the prior can't be authored and can't drift, the ship doesn't
     sail.
- **Wear the lens on ONE existing template** to prove residual is a LENS not a 9th scheme (§R.6):
   take the strike that survived farthest (Mesh / scheme 2 field) and make its atoms cost ink by
    surprise. If a normal atom recedes to texture and a wounded one seizes the eye WITHIN that
     template, the reconciliation is real.

The far water (§R.8) — the prior's cold-start, the churn time-constant, whether "kinship = shared
 prior" holds across referring-vs-holding — is still unproven. Say so; don't crown it yet.

### The cross-cut that decides the ranking: the RESIDUAL lens

The meditation's decisive question, applied to each: *does it compress by SURPRISE (expected recedes
 to near-nothing, only deviation costs ink → scales) or by cleverness that still spends ink per
  particle (→ drowns)?*

**Every one of the eight compresses by cleverness, not by surprise.** Each spends ink per particle,
 by construction — a cell, a band, a tile, a staff, a mote, a stripe-cluster. The cleverest ones
  (Mesh's mote, field-2's density-width, Weave's braid) push the per-particle cost DOWN, which is
   why they sail farther — but none pushes the cost of an *expected* particle to *zero*. That is the
    single axis the task says to rank on, and it is unanimous: **none of the eight has a scale
     answer; they have scale *deferrals*.** They differ only in how far the deferral gets them.

### Ranking by which survives farthest

1. **Mesh (2)** — farthest. Depth-band × mainkey-cluster is honest arithmetic that already GROUPS
    the expected (all `%req` in one steel tangle), which is a half-step toward "expected recedes."
     The mote is the closest any strike comes to a residual. Cliff: the mote still costs ink.
2. **Fibre-band field (4)** — the subtree-weight width channel is a real second dimension of
    compression (breadth without drawing each leaf) and its snap-isomorphism invariant is the
     honesty gold standard. Cliff: still draws every band; density-as-feature is the naive bet named.
3. **Weave (3)** — the braid + minimap is the best *navigation* answer, which buys scale-of-USE even
    though it doesn't buy scale-of-INK. Cliff: a better-indexed scroll is still a scroll.
4. **Three-zone blob (4-file, strike 6)** — best BUNDLE + HONESTY primitives; these two ideas are
    the ones the synthesis KEEPS. Cliff: ink grows with sc-richness AND nesting.
5. **Musical staff (strike 7)** — best DOCTRINE ("lying is structurally prohibited") + the .c
    undertone + the seven-glyph vocabulary. Cliff: overview = a mote by another name, ungrouped.
6. **CelloScore (strike 3)** — the honesty-marks-in-the-text-column idea is elegant and portable.
    Cliff: it is the snap, and the snap is already too long.
7. **Woven Membrane (strike 8)** — richest per-particle reading, O(particles×keys) ink. Cliff: the
    stripe forest; scales worst.
8. **Tree (strike 1)** — the purest containment reading, but 75%-inset nesting sinks fastest into
    the truncation floor. Cliff: depth eats the cells; you navigate, never see.

*(Note the near-tie among 3–7: they are all one-glyph-per-particle fields differentiated by which
 secondary channel they spend on — lineage, density, doctrine, geometry, notation. That near-tie is
  itself the finding: they are variations of ONE template family, not eight distinct answers.)*

## Round 2 — synthesis + push (forbidden from crowning a winner)

### The ONE mechanism all eight are groping toward

Every strike, underneath its metaphor, is trying to make **the expected cost less than the
 surprising**. Read them again with that lens and they stop being rivals:

- Mesh clusters `%req` so a rank of them reads as one motif — *trying to make repetition cheap.*
- The fibre-band field widens rich subtrees and narrows leaves — *trying to spend ink where the
   structure is, not everywhere.*
- The Weave dims deeper tiles and threads a braid — *trying to let the eye skip the ordinary.*
- The three-zone blob draws a TWIST hatch **only when structure was bent** and calls its ABSENCE a
   guarantee — *this is already residual rendering, restricted to one channel (distortion).*
- The staff scheme says "no distortion mark = a faithful transcription" — *the same move.*
- Shunya, in the meditation, names it outright: *draw only what departs; the expected verges on
   invisible.*

So the one mechanism is: **draw the RESIDUAL — the difference between a particle and its
 prediction — and spend ink in proportion to surprise.** The eight strikes each found a *place* to
  put ink (a cell, a band, a stripe) but kept spending it on the expected. The mechanism they're
   missing is the *rule for how much* ink: not "one glyph per particle" but "ink ∝ deviation."

This is exactly predictive coding: the cortex ships the prediction DOWN and only the ERROR travels
 UP. A consciousness holds a vast field because it perceives the *difference* between the world and
  its model of the world, not the world. That is the compression none of the eight has, and the one
   the owner's "sail very far" demands.

### Does "residual as a LENS" hold? (the meditation's strong hunch)

**Yes — and the adversarial pass strengthens it rather than weakening it.** The claim to test:
 residual is not a 9th competing scheme; it is a LENS every template can WEAR — foveal-lossless +
  peripheral-residual, exactly like the eye (sharp centre, gist edge). Three reasons it holds:

1. **Every strike already has a "full glyph" and a "reduced glyph."** Mesh has atom↔mote; the field
    has band↔hairline; the staff has staff↔1px-rule; the blob has strata↔collapsed. The lens simply
     says: *choose which one to draw by SURPRISE, not by zoom.* A normal particle draws the reduced
      glyph (mote/rule/faint texture); a surprising one draws the full glyph (atom/staff/blazing
       cell). The template's own vocabulary supplies both ends; residual only picks between them.
       Nothing new to draw — a new *rule* for what to draw.

2. **It reconciles the "snap IS the picture" convergence (strikes 1, 2, 5-file, and the field) with
    the scale objection.** Snap-as-picture is right *at the fovea* — lossless where attention lands —
     and wrong *everywhere at once* (the naive reading that drowns). Residual makes the snap the
      picture only foveally; the periphery is the residual field. The convergence of the strikes is
       thereby *reconciled, not crowned*: they were all correct about the fovea and naive about the
        periphery.

3. **It collapses HONESTY and COMPRESSION into one mechanism** — which the adversarial pass rates
    the single most valuable property in the whole set. Strike 6's TWIST-absent-is-a-guarantee and
     strike 7's "no mark = faithful transcription" are *already the residual rule applied to the
      distortion channel*. Generalise it: a "twist for effect" is a deliberate deviation from the
       prior, so it blazes by the very rule that compresses everything else — and an intentional
        twist (`sc.twist:'effect'`) simply *shifts the prior* so a by-design warp predicts itself and
         stays calm, while an undeclared wound screams. You cannot hide a distortion because
          distortion is the only thing the renderer spends ink on. The honesty channel every strike
           bolted on (dashed strokes, amber warps, ↑ badges, hatch marks) becomes the *substrate*.

The one caveat the adversarial pass insists on: residual is a lens, **not a levitation spell**. At
 the fovea it is still lossless, so a fovea holding 10⁴ *surprising* particles still drowns. The bet
  is that surprise is RARE by construction (a healthy machine is mostly expected), so the surprising
   set stays small. If a run is pathological — everything wounded at once — the field blazes
    everywhere and residual buys nothing. That is a real failure mode, named in §R.8, not hidden.

### The durable core (what to KEEP from the eight, as inputs to the lens)

The synthesis is not "throw out the eight and build residual." It is "the eight supply the
 templates; residual supplies the ink rule." The concretely durable pieces:

- **The fibre-bundle geometry** (strike 6): rim=mainkey, body=sc, waterline=.c. This is the FOVEAL
   unfold — when a residual mote is surprising enough to bloom, it blooms into *this*.
- **The mote / reduced glyph** (strike 2): the cheapest peripheral form. This is what an *expected*
   particle recedes to.
- **The mainkey-cluster grouping** (strike 2): kinship = shared prior = shared faint texture. This
   is how "expected recedes" reads spatially — a rank of expected `%req` is ONE motif, not N motes.
- **The subtree-weight width channel** (strike 4): a way to show structural mass without drawing
   every leaf — a *pre-computed residual of breadth*.
- **The braid + minimap** (strike 3): the navigation layer, so the fovea can be MOVED cheaply.
- **The TWIST-as-guarantee doctrine** (strikes 6 & 7): the honesty rule that residual generalises.
- **The seven-glyph sc vocabulary** (strike 7): the exhaustive foveal alphabet, incl. the mint-bug
   marks (undef, object-in-sc).

## R — the far-sailing residual design, concretely

### R.1 The core rule

For every particle, compute a scalar **surprise** ∈ [0,1] = a cheap distance between the particle
 and its mainkey's PRIOR. Then **ink ∝ surprise**:

- `surprise ≈ 0` → the peripheral form: a faint mote in a field of its kin, or absorbed entirely
   into its cluster's texture. Costs almost no ink.
- `surprise` moderate → a partial bloom: mote grows a rim label + a few *deviant* sc pills (only the
   keys that differ from the prior, not all keys).
- `surprise ≈ 1` → full foveal unfold: the strike-6 bundle glyph, blazing, dissonant, at its known
   address.

Attention (hover / tap / the current main) forces `surprise := 1` locally — the fovea is lossless on
 demand regardless of prediction. Everything else is drawn at its intrinsic surprise.

### R.2 What the PRIOR is, per mainkey

A prior for mainkey `M` is a compact expected shape, learned from the population of `%M` particles:

- **Expected sc key-set**: which keys a typical `%M` carries (e.g. `%Record` → `id, path, album?`).
- **Expected sc value-shapes**: per key, a coarse type/length band (path-ish, id-ish, flag-only,
   numeric) — NOT the value itself, the *shape*.
- **Expected .c weight**: a typical count band (e.g. a live `%req` mid-flight carries ~2 refs).
- **Expected child fanout**: a typical `o({})` count band.
- **Referring-vs-holding shape**: a `%Card,id:X` (referring, 1:1) has a different expected shape
   than a `%Record,id:X` (holding) even sharing `id`; a `%Spin,of:X` (many:1) expects an `of:`
    pointer. The prior is keyed by mainkey AND by the reference role (`of:` present → referring),
     so a holding masquerading as a reference (the old-magazine bug) is *surprising by construction*.

### R.3 Where the prior lives (matter, beside Matstyle)

A **`%Norm:<mainkey>`** particle under `The/Styles`, sibling to Matstyle's `matstyle:<key>` swatches
 — the same shelf that already holds per-mainkey visual priors (colour), now holding per-mainkey
  *structural* priors (shape). It stores its expectation as `%style:*`/`%meta:*` children C-within-C
   (the Matstyle idiom), NOT flat sc keys, so it snaps cleanly and is hand-editable. Reusing the
    Matstyle shelf is the whole point: *the prior is a swatch for structure the way the jewel is a
     swatch for colour.* It rides in `H.ave` beside This and the swatches; the graph stays clean.

Snap discipline (CLAUDE.md): the `%Norm` never stamps a maybe-undefined sc value (that's the undef
 mint-bug it exists to *detect*); booleans ride as `1`-or-absent; changes go through a C method so
  the snap stays consistent.

### R.4 How deviation is a CHEAP distance

Surprise must be O(1) per particle or the renderer itself doesn't scale. A cheap composite:

```
surprise(n) =  w_k · jaccard_miss(keys(n.sc), Norm.keys)        // wrong/missing/extra sc keys
             + w_v · Σ shape_mismatch(n.sc[k], Norm.shape[k])    // value-shape off its band
             + w_c · band_dist(|n.c|, Norm.c_band)               // .c weight off its band
             + w_f · band_dist(childcount(n), Norm.fanout_band)  // fanout off its band
             + w_x · wound_terms(n)                              // hard wounds → forced high
```

`wound_terms` forces `surprise → 1` on the real distortions CLAUDE.md names: an `undef` sc marker,
 an object/function value in `.sc` (fatal at encode), a `%req,finished` that wasn't dropped, a
  many:1 shape wearing a holding's mainkey, `.c` foam far above the band. These are not soft
   deviations — they are the wounds, and they must blaze. An intentional `sc.twist:'effect'` shifts
    the prior (adds the expected deviation to `Norm`), so a by-design warp yields `surprise ≈ 0` and
     stays calm — the exact discriminator the task demands between intentional twist and wound.

All terms are set/count math over the particle's OWN sc/.c — no tree walk per particle, no physics.
 One pass over the scan the templates already do.

### R.5 The smallest buildable slice (proves "normal vanishes, distorted blazes, same rule")

The one experiment that decides the whole voyage. Build in isolation, no field, no layout:

1. A `%Norm:Record` authored by hand under `The/Styles` (expected keys `{id, path}`, `.c` band 0–2,
    fanout 0).
2. `surprise(n)` as the O(1) composite above (R.4), pure function, unit-testable.
3. `<ResidualMote n H />`: renders a particle whose ink is driven by `surprise` —
   `opacity`, `scale`, and `bloom` (how many deviant sc pills show) all interpolate off it
    (`dose_drives`-style, Matstyle's existing size interpolation).
4. Render three `%Record`s side by side in the Otro dev route:
   - a **normal** one (`{id, path}`) → a near-invisible faint mote,
   - a **wounded** one (`{id, path:undefined}`, the undef mint-bug) → blazes to full glyph,
   - a **foamed** one (normal sc but `.c` far over band) → blooms partially.
   All three by the SAME ink rule, differing only in `surprise`. If the owner sees the normal one
    recede and the wounded one seize the eye, *by one rule*, the mechanism is proven and the ship
     has a keel. If they look the same, the prior or the distance is wrong — fix that before
      anything else.

### R.6 How it composes with the render seam (lens, not scheme)

Residual wears the existing seam verbatim — it is the reason it's a lens not a rival:

- **Registration**: no new `UI:` particle *required*. Residual is a mode-flag on whichever template
   glass is live (`w:Cello / weave:1`-style flag, strike 3's pattern), OR a standalone
    `UI:'CelloResidual'` for the isolated proof. The lens picks reduced-vs-full glyph; the template
     supplies both glyphs.
- **Face mount**: unchanged — `mk = keys(row.sc)[0]`; `Face = GLASS_KINDS[row.sc.face ?? FACE_MAINKEYS[mk]]`;
   `source = row.c.source_n ?? row`; `<svelte:boundary><Face n={source} H={H}/></svelte:boundary>`.
    A foveal unfold of a face-bearing particle mounts its designed Face; a peripheral mote does not
     (a mote is cheaper than a Face, which is the point).
- **Charm**: `cello_blob` for the bloomed glyph, Matstyle jewel for hue, the strike-6 rim/waterline
   for the unfolded bundle. No new primitive except `surprise()` and the `%Norm` lookup.
- **Scan**: the same `Travel`/`H.version`-driven walk every template already does; residual adds one
   O(1) surprise term per row.

The concrete recommendation: **wear the lens on Mesh (strike 2)**, the strike that survived farthest.
 Mesh already groups the expected into clusters; make an *expected* atom recede into its cluster's
  texture (cost ~0) and a *surprising* atom bloom out of it (full atom). That single change turns
   Mesh's "wall of motes" cliff (its one weakness) into open water — the direct test of the whole thesis.

### R.7 The churn answer (residual has a time-constant)

The belief loop bumps constantly; a static residual would flicker. Borrow Rasa's dissonance-resolving:
 a particle that JUST changed is surprising for a beat, then habituates — `surprise` carries a decay,
  so a fresh change blazes then settles to its intrinsic residual over a grawave-style duration. This
   is *also* how "living matter" reads as alive without churning into noise: motion is the derivative
    of surprise, not raw version-bumps. One time-constant, no per-tick relayout.

## R.8 The far water (what still needs proving — do NOT crown residual yet)

1. **Cold-start / the prior's chicken-and-egg.** Before any `%Norm:M` exists, everything is maximally
    surprising and the field blazes everywhere — residual buys nothing on a fresh boot. Needs: a
     bootstrap that learns priors from the first snap (the running machine IS the training set), a
      grace period, and a hand-authorable default. Unproven that this converges to "mostly quiet"
       fast enough to be useful, and unproven it doesn't oscillate as it learns.
2. **The pathological-run failure mode.** Residual's whole scale bet is that surprise is RARE. A
    broken run where everything is wounded blazes everywhere and drowns exactly like the naive
     schemes. Needs: a graceful degradation (when >X% of the field is surprising, the surprise itself
      becomes the expected — a second-order residual, "this world is uniformly broken" as one calm
       signal). Unbuilt, and possibly the hardest part.
3. **Does "kinship = shared prior" survive the full vocabulary?** The clean cases (`%req`, `%Record`)
    share a prior obviously. But referring-vs-holding, compounds, and the House/world/actor
     structural mainkeys (`H:`/`w:`/`A:`) may each need their own prior *family*, and it's unproven
      that a per-mainkey `%Norm` is the right granularity vs. per-role or per-shelf. If a `%Card`
       under a `%Library` expects a different shape than a `%Card` elsewhere, the prior must be keyed
        by CONTEXT, not just mainkey — which is a real complication the isolated slice won't reveal.

The keel is sound: the eight strikes are one template family, and the mechanism they lack is
 render-the-residual, and it is a lens they can all wear. But the prior — its birth, its drift, its
  granularity — is the far water, and it is where the voyage is actually won or lost.
